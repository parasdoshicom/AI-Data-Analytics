Optional carrier exercise · synthetic dispatch log · as of the end of day 12

Operations can keep tomorrow’s shipments on the current carrier or move them to the backup for one day. The backup costs $800 more. The service agreement triggers a switch below 85% on time. Exactly 85% meets the target. Give the operations lead a first recommendation in ten minutes. Then use the rest of this exercise to turn your analysis into a rule another analyst can run.

The warehouse dashboard defines “on time” per parcel. Each row is a delivery scan. An identical scan can arrive twice. The export covers every parcel for the orders shown. Count only promises already due. A due parcel with no delivery by its promise would count as late.

1. Work from dispatch.csv alone. Write down the unit, numerator, denominator, your provisional carrier choice and one question for the contract owner. Save the call before you open the owner’s receipt.
2. Ask the owner what the customer was promised. Open dispatch-owner.txt. Use the same rows and deadline. Record exactly which orders, if any, changed your recommendation.
3. Give the dispatcher an instruction for tomorrow. Which specific rows and which sentence in the agreement would you show someone who disagreed?

4. Make a working copy of decision-brief.md and write the rule in it. Include the deadline, who can approve the spend, what you count once, what you leave out and how you handle exactly 85%. Give the brief and the original CSV to a colleague or a fresh agent and ask for the final counts and the one-day instruction.

5. Once you have both calls, open dispatch-review.txt. Use its two known answers as your answer key. Make small copies of the CSV. Add an identical scan to one and an order due on day 14 to another. Predict whether the decision should change before rerunning your rule. If either copy changes tomorrow’s recommendation, fix the rule and test it again. Also try a hypothetical week with 17 of 20 due orders on time. What does the 85% boundary allow?

6. Write the one sentence you would send the contract owner before the next dispatch. Name who checks the next group of due orders, what evidence they need and who decides whether to pay another $800.
